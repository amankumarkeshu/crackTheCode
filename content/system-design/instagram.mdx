Instagram is mostly News Feed plus WhatsApp plus YouTube stitched together with photo-specific tweaks. If you have already designed those, this question is about identifying which patterns you reuse and which subsystems are truly unique to Instagram. The unique parts: the photo upload pipeline, Stories (a completely different storage strategy), and Explore (interest-based ranking with no follow-graph dependency).

## 1. Clarifying Scope First

- Are we building photos only, or short videos and Reels too?
- Do people follow celebrities who have millions of followers, or is it mostly friends following friends?
- Are Stories 24-hour posts that disappear, and do they live separately from the main feed?
- Do we need a discovery tab showing content from people the user does not follow?
- Is direct messaging in scope?
- Is this global with hundreds of millions of users?

For this article: photos and short videos, full feed with asymmetric follows, 24-hour Stories, Explore tab, direct messaging, 2 billion monthly active users, 100 million posts per day.

## 2. Requirements

**Functional:**
1. Upload a photo or video.
2. See a feed of posts from people you follow.
3. View and post 24-hour Stories.
4. Discover content in the Explore tab.
5. Send and receive direct messages.
6. Like, comment, and save posts.

**Non-Functional:**
1. Feed reads should feel instant: under 200ms p99.
2. Uploaded photos available to followers within seconds.
3. CDN serves about 1.7 million image requests per second globally.
4. Stories disappear at exactly 24 hours.

## 5. High Level Design

```
Mobile App ─► API Gateway ─► Service Mesh ─►

   Posts Service       Media Pipeline (Kafka + workers)
   Feed Service        Object Storage + CDN
   Stories Service     Notification Service
   DM Service          Explore Service (ML)
   User Service        Search Service

Each service owns its data, exposes gRPC APIs.
```

The key split: **Posts** and **Stories** look similar from the outside but use completely different storage strategies because Stories disappear in 24 hours while Posts live forever.

## 6. Deep Dives

### 6.1 Photo upload pipeline

```
1. Client calls init, gets a pre-signed upload URL
2. Client PUTs original photo directly to S3 (chunked for large files)
3. Client calls publish
4. Posts Service persists row (status: PROCESSING), publishes to Kafka
5. Media workers:
   a. Generate 4 sizes: 150x150 thumb, 320, 720, 1080
   b. Strip EXIF metadata (privacy)
   c. Re-encode to WebP/AVIF for size savings
   d. Run safety classifier (NSFW / spam)
   e. Write all variants to object storage
6. Posts Service marks post READY, triggers fan-out to followers' feeds
7. CDN pre-warm: push smallest 2 variants to top regions immediately
```

Why pre-generate sizes rather than resize on the fly? At 1.7 million image reads per second, pre-generated sizes give a dramatically higher CDN cache hit rate. On-the-fly resize is great for low-traffic products; it is economically painful at Instagram scale.

### 6.2 The feed: reuse News Feed pattern

Instagram's home feed uses the same **hybrid push/pull** pattern described in [News Feed (Twitter)](/blog/system-design/design-news-feed-twitter):
- Normal users (median ~150 follows) get fan-out on write: posts go into followers' `FeedInbox`.
- Celebrities (Cristiano Ronaldo with 600M followers) get fan-out on read: pulled at query time.
- Inbox capped at ~500 entries; deep scroll falls back to pull.

The major Instagram-specific difference: **the feed is ranked, not chronological**. The inbox stores candidate post IDs. A ranking layer scores them by relevance at read time and returns them in a personalized order.

### 6.3 Stories: fundamentally different from posts

Stories expire in 24 hours. This changes everything about the storage strategy.

```sql
stories(
  id           bigint PK,
  user_id      bigint,
  media_url    text,
  created_at   timestamp,
  expires_at   timestamp,   -- created_at + 24h
  view_count   bigint
)
```

- **No inbox for Stories.** They use fan-out on read. When you open the app, Instagram queries "stories from people I follow where `expires_at > now()`."
- **No need to push.** Most stories are viewed within hours of posting so the hot read cache covers them efficiently.
- A nightly GC job deletes expired media from object storage.

Why different from posts? Story volume is high but lifetime is tiny. Pre-writing stories to 500M followers' inboxes would be almost entirely wasted effort since 90% would expire before the follower even looks.

This contrast between posts (fan-out on write) and stories (fan-out on read) is a great staff-level point: same product, two completely different storage strategies based on data lifetime and access pattern.

### 6.4 Explore: discovery without the follow graph

The Explore tab shows posts from people you do not follow. It is a pure ranking problem:

```
1. Candidate generation (offline, refreshed hourly):
   For each user, produce ~5K candidate posts based on:
     - Friends-of-friends graph
     - Topic and interest embeddings
     - Geographic affinity
     - Content trending in their language

2. Online scoring (per request, < 100ms):
   Score each candidate with a neural network
   Filter: already-seen, blocked accounts, NSFW
   Return top 20

3. Real-time updates:
   User engages (like, save, long dwell) → update interest embedding
   → next request uses fresh signal
```

### 6.5 Like and follower counts

These are distributed counters with a massive write rate. See [Design a Distributed Counter](/blog/system-design/design-distributed-counter) for the full design.

Short version: each counter is sharded into N sub-counters. Writes go to a random shard; reads sum across shards. Hot posts (a celebrity post going viral) use more shards.

### 6.6 CDN strategy

Photo URLs are content-hashed:
```
https://cdn.ig/p/<sha256-of-content>/<resolution>.webp
```

This means they are infinitely cacheable with no invalidation needed. When a photo is "deleted," it is removed from object storage; the CDN TTL expires it within hours.

<Callout type="tip" title="The 30-second summary">
Instagram is **News Feed (with ranking) plus Chat plus YouTube** with photo-specific tweaks. The unique parts: a multi-resolution upload pipeline driven by Kafka, Stories with fundamentally different storage (TTL-based, fan-out on read), and an Explore page that is pure ML ranking with no follow-graph dependency. Like-counts are distributed counters; everything is content-hashed for infinite CDN cacheability.
</Callout>
